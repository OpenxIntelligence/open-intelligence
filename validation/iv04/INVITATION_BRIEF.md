# Open Intelligence — proposed independent review

**DRAFT — NOT SENT. Recipient, disclosure approval, scope and participation are not established.** This is a proposed one-page invitation for use only after the activity-specific authorization in the accompanying checklist.

## Purpose

Open Intelligence is developing a source-linked oncology investigation workspace for patients and qualified care teams. The current local demonstration uses fictional records: it preserves source versions, shows chronology and uncertainty, records human-authored investigation drafts, and flags dependent work when a report or fictional evidence item changes. It is not a clinical service, treatment recommender, validated medical-document extractor, or substitute for professional judgment.

We propose a bounded independent critique of one capability, starting with permitted non-confidential material. The aim is to find errors, misleading certainty, unnecessary work and invalid assumptions—not obtain an endorsement. Scope, effort, compensation if applicable, and publication/confidentiality expectations would be agreed before participation.

## One question for each review role

**Clinical informatics / medical oncology (E0–E1).** When a fictional report is corrected or arrives late, can a reviewer identify the exact source, specimen, relevant dates, changed assertions and affected prior draft without confusing untested, pending, negative or indeterminate findings? Requested return: a source-anchored discrepancy list, unresolved questions and the effort required to inspect and correct the case.

**Biological / evidence research (E2–E3).** Does the workspace preserve contradictory evidence and applicability limits without turning a citation, computational hypothesis or unknown criterion into evidence of patient benefit or confirmed trial eligibility? Requested return: supported versus unsupported interpretations with source anchors and missing evidence. Fictional studies cannot establish real scientific validity.

**Patient advocacy / human factors (E4–E5).** Can a reader distinguish what the source says, what remains unknown and what needs review, without mistaking demo acknowledgment for clinical approval? Requested return: misunderstood wording, barriers to source inspection, accessibility concerns and observed task burden. No personal health information or medical advice is requested.

**Experimental translational research (future GX only).** What separately specified hypothesis, specimen rights, controls, assay-quality criteria and independent replication would be needed to test a proposed biological mechanism? Requested return now: feasibility questions and missing prerequisites only. No samples, wet-lab work or patient intervention are requested or authorized.

## What would be provided

For an authorized session: a frozen software/packet revision, permitted source materials, exact question and limitations, and a way to report negative findings. A scored comparison would require its own agreed protocol and equivalent manual/simple-software baseline. The current public practice cases and reference key are not blinded or held-out material; prior exposure must be disclosed, and confirmatory assessment needs fresh, separately controlled cases.

Participation would not imply institutional partnership, endorsement, clinical validation or permission to use real patient data. Please first assess whether the question fits your expertise and which participation pathway your institution considers appropriate. A non-confidential scoping conversation and a formal study are different activities.
