# Open Intelligence: biomedical source atlas and rights register

Research date: 2026-11-02 (UTC). Scope: a commercial, first-party cancer-care evidence platform whose MVP retrieves permitted evidence, compares supporting and conflicting findings, explains applicability and uncertainty, produces source-linked reports for human review, and preserves versioned reassessment. This is an engineering rights register, not a claim that any license has been obtained. Only public documentation and policy pages were researched; no patient records, patient datasets, protected accounts, or named-patient research were accessed. No external messages were sent. API routes below are documentation-verified, not runtime integration-tested.

## Executive decisions

1. Start with bibliographic metadata from PubMed, Crossref and OpenAlex; exact-version PMC articles with verified CC0 or CC BY licenses; CIViC-authored knowledge; ClinVar assertions; NCIt; and NLM-created RxNorm names and identifiers. Retain source versions, license evidence and attribution at ingestion.
2. Treat abstracts, full text, figures, supplementary files, terminology imports, and proprietary knowledge as separate rights-bearing objects. A public API, free account, subscription, or `is_oa=true` flag does not establish commercial processing, storage, display, embedding or training rights.
3. Keep NCCN, general ASCO/ESMO guideline ingestion, OncoKB and COSMIC disabled until the applicable written commercial license and AI-use scope are recorded. An individual open-license article can be assessed separately from a society's wider guideline collection.
4. Resolve ClinicalTrials.gov's specific current terms before approving an unrestricted commercial corpus: the terms page did not render in this research, while the official data.gov catalog carries an ODbL label. DailyMed's API is real; unrestricted reuse of all manufacturer-supplied label content and images was not established.
5. Prefer rights-cleared retrieval over training for the MVP. Training, evaluation, RAG, embedding/indexing, transient LLM processing, quotations, report display and archival retention each need their own permission decision.

## Status vocabulary

- **READY — bounded:** Documentation supports the stated access/content scope, subject to the recorded conditions. Not a blanket approval of every field or downstream use.
- **LICENSE REQUIRED:** Official terms explicitly require permission or a license for the proposed commercial use.
- **UNRESOLVED:** Required production terms, rights scope, or conflicting statements remain unverified; deny the affected operation until resolved.

## Fail-closed connector and per-object register

Store permissions as `allowed | denied | unknown`, never as a default `true`. Authorize a specific operation only when both connector-level terms and object-level rights permit it; `unknown`, expired grants and conflicts block that operation. Distinguish a connection's technical authentication from the organization's legal entitlement.

| Identity and provenance | `source_id`, `provider`, `connector_version`, `endpoint`, `retrieved_at`, `upstream_id`, `upstream_version`, `publication_version`, `content_sha256`, `canonical_url`, `source_release`, `source_updated_at` |

Implementation recommendations:

- Make separate source objects for each edition, version and asset. Inherit restrictive exclusions to derived chunks, embeddings, cached snippets and reports; keep reverse links for expiry/removal workflows.
- Keep restricted content outside the retrieval index until approved. Index absence should produce an explicit coverage limitation in the report, not a fabricated synthesis or “no contradictory evidence” claim.
- Preserve exact evidence spans used for a claim where storage rights allow. Otherwise retain IDs, versions, hashes and a permitted locator, with the resulting reproducibility limit shown to reviewers.
- Keep patient identity and raw clinical documents out of public evidence queries. Derive minimum necessary normalized search concepts only from authorized context; apply a specific egress policy before sending them to third parties.
- Trigger review on publication corrections/retractions, new source releases, changed assertions, changed licenses/terms and changed authorized patient context. Reassessment creates a new report version with an evidence delta; it does not silently replace the reviewed report.

## Open questions before enabling affected connectors

1. NCCN: get the current official EULA and organization-specific AI/commercial agreement; establish whether an API, feed or versioned file delivery is available and permitted. No approval inferred.
2. ASCO/ESMO: request organization-wide machine-readable delivery and AI/RAG/storage/report terms; distinguish publisher rights from society permissions. No general guideline API verified.
3. ClinicalTrials.gov: retrieve current terms in an accessible official format and reconcile catalog ODbL; record sponsor-document/attachment exceptions and attribution/currency requirements.
4. DailyMed: confirm which label text, images and attachments may be stored, processed by LLM subprocessors, excerpted and displayed commercially; do not confuse government hosting with government authorship.
5. Europe PMC: recheck production API docs/terms and current delivery routes before deployment; staging/dev pages were corroborating evidence only.
6. OncoKB/COSMIC: negotiate retention needed for reproducible reports, embeddings/RAG, report/export fields, subprocessor processing, end-user licensing and reassessment; do not train on either merely because an API/download is available.
